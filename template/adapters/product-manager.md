# Adapter: product manager

## Tool defaults
Chat (Slack or Teams), mail and calendar (Google or Microsoft), tickets (Linear, Jira or Asana), docs (Google Docs, Notion or Confluence), analytics (PostHog, Amplitude or Mixpanel), whiteboard (Miro or FigJam), meeting notes (Granola, Fireflies or the meeting tool's transcripts).

## Routine emphasis
- `weekly`: roadmap and project health from tickets, OKR progress, insights posts and customer feedback channels as recurring inputs.
- `brief`: ticket mentions and open decisions first, then stakeholders waiting on an answer.
- `review`: learn the style of PRDs, status updates and stakeholder messages separately.
- `radar`: tickets and mentions weigh more than code; skip the code section unless the user works in PRs.

## Glossary seeds
- **PRD**: product requirements document
- **OKR**: objectives and key results
- **RICE**: reach, impact, confidence, effort; a prioritisation score
- **Discovery**: research that decides what to build
- **GA**: general availability, the full release
- **Beta**: limited release to a subset of users

## Typical meeting types
Product sync, roadmap review, stakeholder 1:1, customer interview, sprint planning, design review, launch readiness, quarterly planning.

## Default areas (setup proposes, you confirm)
- **Product:** what we build, why, for whom. Strategy, roadmap, discovery, pricing, insights.
- **Delivery:** shipping with engineering and design. Specs, launches, sprint questions, release readiness.
- **Team:** people, if you lead any. Hiring, 1:1s, feedback.
- **Company:** up and across. Management, budget, go-to-market, other units.
- **Admin:** contracts, invoices, access, expenses.
- **Me:** own growth and outside presence. Talks, writing, learning.

Example tie-break: 1 about a specific person: Team. 2 for someone outside product and tech: Company. 3 what or why we build: Product. 4 how it ships: Delivery. 5 pure bureaucracy: Admin. 6 for yourself: Me.
