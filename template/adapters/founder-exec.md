# Adapter: founder or executive

## Tool defaults
Mail and calendar (Google or Microsoft), chat (Slack or Teams), docs (Google Docs, Notion), CRM or finance dashboards for the numbers you watch, meeting notes (Granola, Fireflies, Meet transcripts), whiteboard. Code and tickets only when you follow them.

## Routine emphasis
- `brief`: decisions waiting on you and people waiting on you first; short. Keep Top of mind to 3-7 initiatives.
- `weekly`: one block per initiative with state, owner and next milestone; the numbers you watch as recurring inputs; plan vs actual for how much of the week went to unplanned work.
- `meeting-prep`: 1:1s with direct reports and board, investor or customer meetings; use the topic block for every initiative review.
- `review`: learn the style of announcements, replies to the team and external mails; keep a separate voice per audience.
- `debrief`: use it; the reflection lenses matter most at this level.

## Glossary seeds
- **OKR**: objectives and key results
- **Runway**: months of cash left at the current burn
- **Burn**: net cash spent per month
- **1:1**: recurring one-on-one meeting
- **All-hands**: company-wide meeting
- **Board pack**: the document sent before a board meeting

## Typical meeting types
Leadership sync, 1:1 with a direct report, board or investor meeting, customer or partner call, all-hands, hiring decision, quarterly planning, offsite.

## Default areas (setup proposes, you confirm)
- **Strategy:** direction, bets, planning, the numbers you watch.
- **Customers:** key customers, partners, investors, market.
- **Team:** people. Hiring, 1:1s, feedback, org design.
- **Company:** operations across the company. Finance, legal, other units.
- **Admin:** contracts, invoices, access, expenses.
- **Me:** own growth and outside presence. Talks, writing, network.

Example tie-break: 1 about a specific person: Team. 2 about a named customer, partner or investor: Customers. 3 direction or the numbers: Strategy. 4 running the company: Company. 5 pure bureaucracy: Admin. 6 for yourself: Me.

## Which people matter (setup's "Map your colleagues")
Relations that matter: the leadership team (direct reports), co-founders and board contacts (stakeholders), key advisers and investors (external). Mark the few people you meet weekly as close. Identities that matter: mail and chat first, then tasks; code and ticket logins only for people who work there. Personnel matters stay in your own words in "How we work".
